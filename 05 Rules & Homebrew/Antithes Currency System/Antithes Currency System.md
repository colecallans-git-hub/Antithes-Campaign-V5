
Value in Antithes is based primarily on **weight and purity**. Bartering of goods is extremely common. When metal is used, people weigh and test it. Coins, bars, jewelry, raw chunks, and cut pieces are all accepted.

Promissory notes issued by **The Red Seal** (backed by the **House of the Golden Quill**) state the metal type and amount, and are sealed with a wax stamp. A fresh seal appears **red**. Over time, or when a due date passes, the seal darkens from the center outward until it turns fully **black**. A black seal indicates the note is overdue.

## Individual Currency Details

### Pit-Iron / Slag

- **Material**: Common, often impure iron

- **Forms**: Rough tokens, scraps, small billets

- **Appearance**: Dull gray, prone to rust if neglected

- **Notes**: The lowest form of pay. Can be forged into basic tools and simple weapons. Rarely accepted on promissory notes.

### Copper / Bronze

- **Material**: Copper and copper-tin alloys

- **Forms**: Coins, scraps, small bars, rings

- **Appearance**: Reddish to warm golden-brown

- **Notes**: The everyday currency of markets and shops. Minimum metal normally accepted on Red Seal promissory notes. Extremely abundant.

### Hacksilver

- **Material**: Silver

- **Forms**: Cut pieces, broken jewelry, rings, small bars

- **Appearance**: Bright, soft metallic silver

- **Notes**: More carefully weighed than copper. Preferred for higher-quality weapons and armor because it resists corrosion better than iron. Still relatively common.  

### Gold

- **Material**: Gold

- **Forms**: Coins, small bars, jewelry

- **Appearance**: Classic warm yellow metal

- **Notes**: Uncommon. Usually seen in the purses of the wealthy or in noble households. Widely recognized and trusted.

### Spire-Silver / Dragon-Silver

- **Material**: Rare natural silver-platinum alloy

- **Forms**: Cut pieces from geodes, small refined ingots, high-value jewelry

- **Appearance**: Brilliant silver with a diamond-like / white-sapphire sparkle. Does not tarnish.

- **Source**: Found inside colorful geodes (pearlescent purple, deep pink, pastel green or blue) roughly the size of a basketball. The outer geode is often used for jewelry; the pure metal within is reserved for high-value trade and banking.

- **Notes**: Extremely rare. Backs major promissory notes issued by The Red Seal. Said to carry minor magical durability.
